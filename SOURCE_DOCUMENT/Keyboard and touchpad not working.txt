Keyboard and touchpad not working
1. Check if the laptop is plugged into a power source: Sometimes, the keyboard and touchpad might stop working when the battery is low or the laptop is running on battery power alone. So, first, make sure that your laptop is connected to a power source and has enough charge.
2. Restart the laptop: A simple restart can often fix minor software issues that might be causing the problem. So, restart your laptop and check if the keyboard and touchpad are working now.
3. Check for physical damage: Make sure that there's no visible damage to the keyboard or touchpad, such as spilled liquids, loose connections, or debris stuck between the keys. If you notice any physical damage, try cleaning it with a soft-bristled brush or compressed air and see if that helps.
4. Disable other input devices: Make sure that there are no other input devices connected to your laptop, such as an external keyboard or mouse, which might be interfering with the built-in ones. Go to the "Device Manager" in Windows and disable any unused input devices listed there.
5. Check for driver updates: Outdated drivers for your keyboard and touchpad can sometimes cause problems. So, go to the manufacturer's website and look for any available driver updates for your laptop model. Alternatively, use a driver updater software to automatically scan for and install any necessary driver updates.
6. Disable third-party software: Sometimes, third-party software like antivirus programs or gaming overlays might conflict with the built-in keyboard and touchpad drivers. Try disabling these programs temporarily and see if that helps. 
7. Reset the laptop: If nothing else works, try resetting your laptop to its factory settings. This will erase all your data, so make sure to back up any important files beforehand. To reset your laptop, go to the "Settings" app, click on "Update & Security," and select "Reset this PC."
8. Contact the manufacturer: If none of the above steps work, it might be a hardware issue with your keyboard or touchpad. In that case, you should contact the laptop's manufacturer for further assistance. They may offer a warranty repair or suggest other solutions based on the specific model and issue at hand.
